# 2. Original-paper atlas

Access checked 2026-10-17. “Public full text” means a public paper or author/institutional manuscript was retrievable; it does not mean a permissive redistribution license, complete replication, or that every page was closely read. Methods and bibliographic headers were checked in extracted text. Search-engine crawl dates are not publication dates.

## Reconstruction without a dedicated runtime recorder

**P01 — Vinay Deolalikar and Hernan Laffitte. “Provenance as data mining: Combining file system metadata with content analysis.” TaPP 2009.** [Original USENIX full text](https://static.usenix.org/events/tapp09/tech/full_papers/deolalikar/deolalikar_html/Cluster_Provenance_final.html). Access: public full text. Method: document content similarity/clustering, filesystem metadata, and temporal ordering to suggest historical document chains. Direct counterexample for legacy files without filesystem enhancements. Its small, selected evaluation and acknowledged difficulty establishing recall do not justify general causal certainty. Borrow candidate generation, not its strong rhetoric about residual similarity or timestamps.

**P02 — Sara Magliacane. “Reconstructing Provenance.” ISWC 2012 doctoral-consortium contribution.** [VU institutional manuscript](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/669793/ISWC2012%20DC.pdf). Access: public peer-reviewed-version manuscript; repository warns against redistribution. Method: metadata/content preprocessing, multiple signal detectors, hypothesis generation, pruning, and ranked plausible reconstructions for shared-folder files. This is a research proposal with preliminary results, not evidence of a mature universal reconstructor. Particularly relevant to retaining competing candidates and domain-specific constraints.

**P03 — Tom De Nies, Sara Magliacane, Ruben Verborgh, Sam Coppens, Paul Groth, Erik Mannens, Rik Van de Walle. “Git2PROV: Exposing Version Control System Content as W3C PROV.” ISWC Posters & Demos 2013, CEUR 1035, pp. 125–128.** [Paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1035/iswc2013_demo_32.pdf); [author bibliography](https://ruben.verborgh.org/publications/denies_iswc_2013/). Access: public paper and bibliographic metadata. Method: clone a repository, inspect Git history, map files/commits/authorship/dependencies into PROV. Demonstrates post-hoc conversion of existing records. Commit history remains narrower than all filesystem activity; mapped attribution is a recorded assertion, not independent identity verification. Historical hosted-service availability was not tested.

**P04 — Timothy McPhillips, Shawn Bowers, Khalid Belhajjame, Bertram Ludäscher. “Retrospective Provenance Without a Runtime Provenance Recorder.” TaPP 2015.** [Paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/tapp15-mcphillips.pdf); [venue record](https://www.usenix.org/conference/tapp15/workshop-program/presentation/mcphillips). Access: public full text. YesWorkflow annotations and URI templates connect prospective blocks to metadata encoded in surviving directory/file organization. Dependencies are inferred under declared conventions, not directly observed. This is a strong counterexample to universal instrumentation claims, but not annotation-free recovery from arbitrary logs. Relevant to domain admission adapters and preserving assumptions alongside inferred edges.

**P05 — Tom Blount, Adriane Chapman, Michael Johnson, Bertram Ludäscher. “Observed vs. Possible Provenance.” TaPP 2021.** [Paper](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/tapp2021_blount.pdf). Access: public full text. Distinguishes direct/post-hoc observation from possible/inferred provenance; illustrates abductive possible histories using Connect Four. It motivates retaining alternatives rather than inventing one chain. The example is not a production artifact-reconstruction benchmark. Whether a true history remains in the candidate set depends on the completeness and correctness of the modeled rules and observations.

## Execution-time collection and reproducibility

**P06 — Kiran-Kumar Muniswamy-Reddy, David A. Holland, Uri Braun, Margo Seltzer. “Provenance-Aware Storage Systems.” USENIX ATC 2006.** [Paper](https://www.usenix.org/event/usenix06/tech/full_papers/muniswamy-reddy/muniswamy-reddy.pdf). Access: public full text. PASS integrates provenance collection with storage/system-level execution. It is a mature counterpoint on capture coverage and coupling data to provenance, not a way to recover unrecorded past activity. File/process dependence also need not mean semantic contribution of every input byte.

**P07 — Leonardo Murta, Vanessa Braganholo, Fernando Chirigati, David Koop, Juliana Freire. “noWorkflow: Capturing and Analyzing Provenance of Scripts.” IPAW 2014 contribution.** [Author manuscript](https://braganholo.github.io/papers/murta2014-ipaw.pdf). Access: public full text. AST analysis, reflection, and profiling collect Python-script provenance without requiring scientists to manually instrument scripts or adopt a workflow engine. Collection still occurs around execution. Distinguish the 2014 workshop contribution from later proceedings publication dates and from Pimentel et al.'s separate 2017 tool paper. Useful for granularity and dependency analysis; not proof that old unobserved runs can be reconstructed.

**P08 — Fernando Chirigati, Dennis Shasha, Juliana Freire. “ReproZip: Using Provenance to Support Computational Reproducibility.” TaPP 2013.** [Paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/tapp13/tapp13-final16.pdf). Access: public full text. OS-call tracking identifies dependencies and packages experiment data, binaries, and environment. The examined paper describes a SystemTap-based implementation; do not silently attribute that mechanism to every current ReproZip release. Useful model for capture manifests and isolated replay. A new successful replay establishes reproducibility under recorded inputs, not automatically that the historical output had those bytes.
