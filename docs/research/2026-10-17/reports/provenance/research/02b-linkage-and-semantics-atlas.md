# 2b. Papers on linkage, semantics, ordering and privacy

Access checked 2026-10-17; public text access does not imply redistribution rights or replicated results. Continued from [Part 2](02-paper-atlas.md).

## Linkage, schema matching, and semantics

**P09 — Ivan P. Fellegi and Alan B. Sunter. “A Theory for Record Linkage.” JASA 64(328), 1969, pp. 1183–1210.** [Public institutional scan](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~shmat/courses/cs6434/fellegi-sunter.pdf). Access: full scanned paper; OCR mangles names, so use the bibliographic spelling above. Comparison patterns and match/nonmatch likelihoods support link, non-link, and unresolved decisions under stipulated error rates. Relevant to calibrated candidate review and explicit abstention. Filename/content similarity scores are not probabilities merely because they lie between zero and one; correlated features and domain shift need evaluation.

**P10 — Erhard Rahm and Philip A. Bernstein. “A survey of approaches to automatic schema matching.” The VLDB Journal 10, 2001, pp. 334–350.** [Author/institutional PDF](https://dbs.uni-leipzig.de/files/research/publications/2001-1/pdf/VLDBJ-Dec2001.pdf). Access: public full text, publication header verified. Taxonomy covers schema/instance, element/structure, linguistic/constraint matching and combinations. Supports adapter-specific mappings with review and ambiguity. Matching fields by name does not establish semantic equivalence, and a correspondence is not an executable migration.

**P11 — Peter Buneman, Sanjeev Khanna, Wang-Chiew Tan. “Why and Where: A Characterization of Data Provenance.” ICDT 2001, LNCS 1973, pp. 316–330.** [Institutional manuscript](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16509989/Why_and_Where_A_Characterization_of_Data_Provenance.pdf); [author PDF](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/opb/papers/ICDT2001.pdf). Access: public paper text through search extraction. Distinguishes why an output exists from where extracted data originated in database queries. Useful conceptual separation between an evidence span and the derivation justifying a catalog assertion. Results for query languages do not solve arbitrary shell or natural-language causality.

**P12 — Todd J. Green, Grigoris Karvounarakis, Val Tannen. “Provenance Semirings.” PODS 2007.** [Author PDF](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf). Access: public paper, text inspected via search/fetch. Annotated relational semantics express alternative and combined derivations algebraically. Useful later if query answers need dependency-aware explanation or invalidation. Not required for a small artifact catalog; polynomial annotations alone do not establish trust, causal truth, or calibrated confidence.

**P13 — Leslie Lamport. “Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System.” CACM 21(7), July 1978, pp. 558–565.** [Author's publication page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/time-clocks-ordering-events-distributed-system/); [paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Time-Clocks-and-the-Ordering-of-Events-in-a-Distributed-System.pdf). Access: author page and paper text indexed; combined fetch did not return the PDF body. Happened-before is a partial order; timestamps alone do not establish causal dependence. Useful for tool call/result pairing and concurrent-writer probes. A total display order is not a unique history.

**P14 — James Cheney and Roly Perera. “An Analytical Survey of Provenance Sanitization.” 2014 preprint; revised proceedings publication 2015.** [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5777); [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.5777); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-16462-5_9). Access: public preprint/full HTML; publisher full-text access not established. Reviews seven approaches to provenance views/redaction and tensions between confidentiality and explanatory integrity. Supports treating provenance metadata itself as sensitive and preserving sanitization provenance. It does not validate an enterprise permission design for Gleaner.

## Reading order

Read P05 → P04 → P03 for the reconstruction boundary; P09/P10 for uncertain identity and mappings; P14 for enterprise disclosure. Read P06–P08 when deciding what minimal prospective capture would close a particular retrospective gap. P11/P12 are useful theory, not an architecture mandate. Papers are evidence of prior art, not packages adopted into Gleaner.
